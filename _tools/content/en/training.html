Training

Dog Sport, Mantrailing & Detection Work

Three training worlds, one core idea: dogs need tasks for both body and nose — not just exercise. All three areas build on Chris Knittel's background in competitive sport, trailing training and professional service-dog work.

Competitive dog sport

Dog sport

THS ("Turnierhundesport", German tournament dog sport) combines obedience, agility and teamwork between dog and handler in a sporting, competition-oriented format. Chris has been an active competitor since 2014 — with SWHV, VDH and DHV national championship starts, more than 70 competition starts, and several podium finishes and honorary awards from the City of Metzingen.

This competitive experience feeds directly into training: solid foundational obedience, team spirit and the joy of achieving something together — whether you want to compete yourself or simply want to give your dog a sporting challenge.

Nose work

Mantrailing (Man/Pet Trail)

In mantrailing, the dog follows the individual scent trail of a specific person (man trailing) or animal (pet trailing) — not a general ground scent, but the unique scent signature of one individual. The dog works largely independently, while the handler learns to read the dog's body language and to trust it.

This is an ideal task, especially for smart, high-energy dogs: focused nose work is often more tiring than physical exercise alone, while also strengthening the bond between dog and handler. The training builds on Chris's nine-month qualification as a Pet/Man Trail instructor with Pet Trailer in Austria.

Experience from professional service-dog work

Detection dogs (Spürhunde)

Through his work as a service dog handler in civilian and military security services, Chris brings additional experience in professional detection-dog work — including the targeted training of alert behaviour and calm, controlled use of the dog's nose under real-world conditions.
